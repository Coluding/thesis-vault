Lesson 1 · conditional variance

Why L2 hands you a ceiling you cannot out-engineer

One derivation, three lines, and the reason your injection-site experiments were never going to separate.

1. The only thing squared error can learn

Take any predictor f of a target Y from an input X. Add and subtract the conditional mean:

E[(Y − f(X))²] = E[( (Y − E[Y|X]) + (E[Y|X] − f(X)) )²] = E[(Y − E[Y|X])²] + E[(E[Y|X] − f(X))²] + 2·E[cross]

The cross term dies. Condition on X first: the second factor is fixed once you know X, and the first has conditional mean zero by construction, so the inner expectation is 0. Hence

E[(Y − f(X))²] = E[Var[Y|X]] + E[(E[Y|X] − f(X))²] └ irreducible ┘ └ your model's error ┘

The second term is the only one you control, and it is minimised at f(X) = E[Y|X]. So squared-error training cannot learn anything but the conditional mean, and whatever spread remains around it is a floor.

This is not a diffusion fact. It is a fact about L2. Diffusion and flow matching are both L2 regressions onto a target (ε, v, or x₀), so both inherit it. That is precisely why it can compare them.

2. Splitting the floor with actions

Now add the action A. Apply the same identity inside the conditioning — this is the law of total variance:

Var[Y | X] = EA[ Var[Y | X, A] ] + VarA( E[Y | X, A] ) └ still irreducible ┘ └ actions explain ┘

Read the two halves as budget. The left is what stays unpredictable even if you knew the action perfectly. The right is the part actions can account for.

3. The ceiling

Compare the two best-possible predictors. Blind: E[Y|X], MSE E[Var[Y|X]]. Action-aware: E[Y|X,A], MSE E[Var[Y|X,A]]. Subtract, and substitute the split above:

best blind MSE − best action-aware MSE = E[ VarA( E[Y | X, A] ) ]
The consequence. That quantity is the entire loss reduction any action-conditioned model can achieve. Not "with this adapter" — ever. It is fixed by the data and the representation before any architecture is chosen.

Which retro-explains a campaign of null results. Cross-attention, channel concat, adaLN, token-norm, gain fixes — all pressed against the same bound, so all landed in the same effect_rel band. Moving where a signal enters cannot raise a ceiling set by how much that signal explains.

4. Explaining away — the part that flips the blind arm

The conditioning is on x_t. If the noisy latent already reveals the action — an arm mid-swing is visible under noise — then given x_t, the action is largely redundant. Not uninformative: pre-empted.

VarA( E[Y | A] ) ≥ VarA( E[Y | X, A] ) blind arm's ceiling normal adapter's ceiling

So the action-only adapter — no base prediction, no x_t — is not the handicapped arm. Its absolute accuracy collapses, but its action-explained share should rise, because nothing is explaining actions away any more.

The adapter's injection site changed from cross-attention to concat, and effect_rel barely moved. What does the decomposition say?

5. Your number, restated

The campaign measured actions worth 0.45% of the teacher-forced denoising loss. In this language that is the ratio of the two halves of §2 — an estimate of VarA(E[Y|X,A]) / Var[Y|X]. It stops being a curious empirical number and becomes a structural one.

What it is not yet is comparable across backbones: DC's loss lives in a 4-channel SD-VAE latent, Wan's in a 48-channel one. Normalising is the next lesson.
