UniCon — hidden-state adapter

Freeze the whole base; capture its internal activations (detached) via forward hooks; retrain a copy of the decoder whose every hidden state is conditioned on those captured features through zero-init connectors. Shown: the decoder-focused variant (UniConHiddenStateAdapter).

Zoom: the zero-init connector (zeroft, the default)

Each connector fuses a captured base activation (the source) into the replica's current hidden state (the target) through both an additive and a multiplicative path, both zero-init. At training step 0 the connector is the identity (out = target), so the adapter starts as an exact pass-through over the frozen base's activations and only departs from it as the connectors learn. The alternative zeroconv is the additive-only (ControlNet-style) connector.

The base runs once, detached; its encoder skips, middle, and decoder activations are captured by forward hooks and enter the adapter as constants. So gradient flows only through the replica decoder and the connectors — the frozen base is never in the backward graph (the heavy part is the replica, a design choice, not a forced base traversal). The adapter returns the shared OutputAdapterResult (prediction + optional per-pixel gate from a copied out_mask head), composed by AdaptedModel exactly like the AVID and HyperAlign families.

Three variants in the hidden-state family (same capture path, different replica size):
VariantTrainable replicaConnectorsRole
UniCon (shown, 3d)decoder + out headzero-init (middle / skip / decoder)the canonical design

Encoder is not replicated in the UniCon / ReplaceDecoder variants — the decoder copy is driven by the frozen encoder's captured skips. See the companion gradient-flow.html for why this keeps backward cheap, and avid.html / hyperalign.html for the neighbouring adapter families.
