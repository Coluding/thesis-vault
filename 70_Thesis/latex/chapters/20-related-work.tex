\section{Preliminaries: diffusion and flow matching}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

This section fixes the notation and the two facts about generative
trajectories that the rest of the thesis relies on, including the few-step
literature reviewed in \cref{sec:rw-few-step}. It is deliberately minimal:
we state what is used later and no more.

\subsection{Forward process and prediction target}

Both model classes we work with define a path between data and noise. Given
a sample $x_0$ and Gaussian noise $\noisepred \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, an
intermediate state is a mixture of the two, indexed by a time $t$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:interpolant}
  \xt = \alpha_t\, x_0 + \sigma_t\, \noisepred ,
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_t$ and $\sigma_t$ set by the schedule. Training samples $t$,
constructs $\xt$, and regresses a network on a target derived from
$(x_0, \noisepred)$.

\emph{Which} target is a choice, and it matters enough that we state it
everywhere it is relevant. A model may predict the noise $\noisepred$, the
clean sample $x_0$, or the velocity $\vel$; the three are related by
invertible transformations at fixed $t$, but the loss surfaces and the
downstream algebra differ. We therefore carry two labels with every model:
\cfg{model\_type}, which is \code{diffusion} or \code{flow}, and
\cfg{prediction\_type}, which is \code{noise}, \code{x0} or
\code{velocity}. All backbones in this thesis use velocity prediction; only
the model type varies. That is an accident of which models were available
rather than a design choice, and \cref{sec:res-pathway} makes use of it.

\subsection{The sampling trajectory, and why its shape matters}

Sampling integrates a deterministic trajectory from noise to data. Its
instantaneous direction is the velocity field, and the geometry of the path
it traces is the property \cref{sec:curvature} turns on.

Under a \textbf{variance-preserving diffusion} schedule, $\alpha_t$ and
$\sigma_t$ vary non-linearly, and the trajectory from noise to data is
\emph{curved}: the velocity at one point of the path does not equal the
velocity at another. We write $\curv$ for that curvature.

Under \textbf{rectified flow matching}, the interpolant of
\cref{eq:interpolant} is taken to be linear in $t$, so the path between a
noise sample and a data sample is a straight line, the velocity is constant
along it, and $\curv = 0$.

This is the whole of the distinction we need. Flow matching is not treated
here as a different family of model but as \emph{the case in which the
sampling trajectory is straight}, and the consequences of that appear in
\cref{sec:shortcut} and \cref{sec:curvature}.

\subsection{Step size}

Sampling proceeds in a finite number of steps. We write $\dstep$ for the
size of a step, that is the interval of $t$ traversed by one network
evaluation, and \emph{few-step sampling} means increasing $\dstep$ so that
fewer evaluations cover the same path. A model that is conditioned on
$\dstep$ can in principle be asked for a step of any size at inference,
which is the property \cref{sec:shortcut} trains for and
\cref{sec:res-shortcut} evaluates.

\section{Adapting frozen generative models}
\label{sec:rw-adapters}

Adapting a large pretrained model without retraining it is by now a standard
practice, and the methods differ mainly in \emph{how much of the base they
need}. We present them in that order, from those that modify the model's
weights to those that require only its output, because the resulting
gradient is what \cref{sec:families} uses to select among them.

\paragraph{Inside the weights.} The original adapters of
\citet{houlsby2019adapters} insert a small bottleneck module into each layer
of a frozen transformer, initialised near the identity so that the model
begins unchanged. \citet{hu2022lora} instead leave the architecture
untouched and learn a low-rank update to the weight matrices themselves,
which can be merged back into the base at inference. Both leave the
pretrained parameters frozen in the sense that they are not updated; both
require the model to be open enough to insert modules into, or to add
matrices to.

\paragraph{Inside the activations.} For conditional image generation the
dominant approach adds a second, trainable pathway that reads and writes the
base's intermediate features. ControlNet~\citep{zhang2023controlnet} makes a
trainable copy of the frozen encoder and feeds its features back into the
frozen network through zero-initialised convolutions, so that the untrained
branch contributes nothing at initialisation and grows from there.
T2I-Adapter~\citep{mou2024t2iadapter} does something lighter, encoding the
condition with a small convolutional network and adding its features to the
frozen encoder's features at several scales.
IP-Adapter~\citep{ye2023ipadapter} adds a parallel set of key and value
projections beside each existing cross-attention layer and sums the two
attention outputs. These methods require access to intermediate activations,
and in ControlNet's case to enough of the architecture to duplicate it.

\paragraph{Outside the model entirely.} AVID~\citep{rigter2024avid} makes
the strongest assumption about availability and the weakest about access. It
treats the pretrained video diffusion model as a black box, using only its
prediction, and trains an adapter that emits both its own prediction and a
mask that blends the two. The base's weights, activations and gradients are
never touched.

\paragraph{A convention worth noticing.} Every method above initialises its
new pathway so that it contributes nothing at the start of training:
near-identity bottlenecks, zero convolutions, zero-initialised output
projections, gated attention with the gate at zero. The motivation is always
the same and always about safety, that an untrained branch should not
disturb a working model. The question of whether such a pathway subsequently
grows to contribute anything is rarely asked; two works reframe the coupling
as a bandwidth problem~\citep{zavadski2024controlnetxs} or replace the
zero-initialisation with a normalisation-based
alternative~\citep{peng2024controlnext}, and neither frames the issue as the
conditioning going unused. We return to this in \cref{sec:res-scale}.
