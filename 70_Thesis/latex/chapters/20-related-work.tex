\chapter{Related work}

\section{Diffusion and flow matching}
\label{sec:rw-generative}

The models this thesis adapts all generate by transporting noise to data
along a learned trajectory. This section introduces the two families that
do so, fixes the notation used throughout, and isolates the two properties
of the trajectory that later chapters depend on.

\subsection{Diffusion models}

Diffusion models were introduced by \citet{sohldickstein2015diffusion} as a
pair of Markov chains: a fixed forward chain that destroys the structure of
the data by repeated Gaussian noising, and a learned reverse chain of the
same functional form that undoes it. Because both directions are Gaussian
for small enough steps, the generative model is tractable to sample and to
evaluate.

\citet{ho2020ddpm} made the construction practical by reparameterising the
variational bound as \emph{noise prediction}: the network is trained to
recover the noise $\noisepred$ that was added to a clean sample $x_0$ to
produce a corrupted state
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:forward}
  \xt = \alpha_t\, x_0 + \sigma_t\, \noisepred ,
  \qquad \noisepred \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I),
\end{equation}
under a simplified loss weighting. Subsequent work refined the schedule and
the learned variances~\citep{nichol2021iddpm} and, in
\citet{karras2022edm}, separated the design into independent axes of
network preconditioning, noise distribution, discretisation and sampler.
The parameterisation used in most current systems follows the latter.

\citet{song2021sde} recast the discrete construction in continuous time as
a stochastic differential equation, with a reverse-time SDE and an
equivalent deterministic \emph{probability-flow ODE} sharing the same
marginals. This is the view we adopt, because it is what allows the second
family below to be introduced as a variation rather than as a separate
object.

\subsection{Flow matching}

Flow matching takes the deterministic view as the starting point.
\citet{lipman2023flowmatching} train a continuous normalising flow without
simulating it, by regressing the network directly on a \emph{conditional}
velocity field defined along a prescribed probability path between noise and
data. Diffusion paths are recovered as one choice of path, and other choices
are available.

The choice that matters here is the linear one. Rectified
flow~\citep{liu2023rectifiedflow} takes the interpolant of
\cref{eq:forward} to be linear in $t$, so that the transport between a noise
sample and a data sample follows a straight line. The same construction is
developed as a general framework by
\citet{albergo2023interpolants, albergo2025stochasticinterpolants}, who show
that the probability-flow ODE and a family of SDEs share a single learned
drift.

\subsection{What distinguishes them, for our purposes}

Presented this way the two families differ in one respect that matters to
this thesis: \textbf{the shape of the sampling trajectory}.

Under a variance-preserving diffusion schedule, $\alpha_t$ and $\sigma_t$
vary non-linearly and the path traced from noise to data is \emph{curved};
the velocity at one point of the path differs from the velocity at another.
We write $\curv$ for that curvature. Under a rectified-flow construction the
path is straight, the velocity is constant along it, and $\curv = 0$.

We therefore treat flow matching not as a different kind of model but as
\emph{the case in which the sampling trajectory is straight}. This is a
narrow reading of a broad literature, and it is the reading the rest of the
thesis needs: \cref{sec:curvature} shows that a widely used training
objective for few-step sampling is exact when $\curv = 0$ and biased
otherwise, and \cref{sec:backbones} selects backbones accordingly.

\subsection{Prediction targets, and why we always name them}

A model on either side of this distinction may be trained to predict the
noise $\noisepred$, the clean sample $x_0$, or the velocity $\vel$. At a
fixed $t$ the three are related by invertible transformations, but the loss
surfaces differ and, more importantly for us, so does the algebra of any
objective built on top of them. Confusing them is the most common way to
misstate a result in this area.

We therefore label every model with two attributes and repeat them wherever
a loss or a target is defined: \cfg{model\_type}, which is \code{diffusion}
or \code{flow}, and \cfg{prediction\_type}, which is \code{noise},
\code{x0} or \code{velocity}. Every backbone in this thesis predicts
velocity; only the model type varies. That is a consequence of which
pretrained models were available rather than a design choice, and
\cref{sec:res-pathway} makes use of it.

\subsection{Sampling cost}

Sampling integrates the trajectory in a finite number of steps, and each
step costs one network evaluation. We write $\dstep$ for the size of a step,
that is the interval of $t$ it traverses, and refer to the total as the
number of function evaluations. Reducing it is the subject of
\cref{sec:rw-few-step}; a model conditioned on $\dstep$ can in principle be
asked for a step of any size at inference, which is the property
\cref{sec:shortcut} trains for.

\section{World models}
\label{sec:rw-world-models}

A world model is a learned model of environment dynamics that an agent can
query instead of acting: given a state and an action, it predicts what
follows. The idea is longstanding~\citep{schmidhuber1990online}, and
\citet{ha2018worldmodels} gave it its modern form, training a compact latent
dynamics model and then evolving a controller entirely inside the model's
own rollouts. \citet{lecun2022path} argues for predictive world models as
the organising principle for autonomous agents more broadly; we cite it for
the framing and note that it is a position paper rather than a
peer-reviewed result. \citet{ding2025worldmodelsurvey} survey the area.

For our purposes the literature is best sorted by a single question:
\textbf{how much of the generative model is trained, and from what?} That
axis is what places the present work, and it separates four regimes.

\paragraph{Learned from scratch, in a compact latent space.} The Dreamer
line learns a recurrent latent dynamics model per environment and trains a
policy on imagined rollouts~\citep{hafner2019planet, hafner2020dreamer,
hafner2021dreamerv2}, reaching a wide range of tasks under a single
configuration in \citet{hafner2025dreamerv3}. Actions condition the latent
transition. These models are cheap per task and carry no visual prior: they
are trained on the environment they will be used in, and transfer nothing
from elsewhere.

\paragraph{Learned from scratch, generatively.} A second group keeps the
full generative machinery but still trains it from
scratch. \citet{alonso2024diamond} train a diffusion world model per Atari
game and show that visual detail matters for the resulting policy.
\citet{bruce2024genie} scale the idea to unlabelled internet video at
eleven billion parameters, and, importantly for us, learn a \emph{latent}
action space rather than consuming ground-truth actions. Both pay the full
training cost, and Genie's action interface is a different problem from
ours: we have action labels and wish to condition on them.

\paragraph{Started from a pretrained model, then fully fine-tuned.} The
most common recent recipe begins from an existing generative model and
updates all of its weights. \citet{valevski2025gamengen} repurpose a
text-to-image diffusion model into a real-time game engine this way, and
\citet{yang2024unisim} train a large video simulator conditioned on both
language and low-level control. This regime inherits the prior and then
overwrites it; it is the natural comparison for anything that claims a
pretrained model is worth preserving.

\paragraph{Pretrained model held frozen, with a trained module.} Finally,
\citet{rigter2024avid} keep the pretrained video diffusion model entirely
fixed, without access to its parameters, and train a small adapter that
modifies its outputs under action conditioning. This is the regime the
present work occupies, and AVID is its direct antecedent rather than a gap
we identify.

\medskip

The distinction between the last two regimes is the one this thesis turns
on. Full fine-tuning is a strictly larger hypothesis class and will, given
enough data and compute, do at least as well. The case for keeping the base
frozen is that the strongest video models are increasingly released as
weights that can be sampled but not retrained; that the prior is what makes
the model worth using at all, so overwriting it is a cost rather than a
neutral choice; and that the resulting module is small enough to be trained
on a domain-specific dataset. \Cref{sec:families} makes the corresponding
architectural argument, and \cref{sec:limitations} states what we give up.

\section{Adapting a pretrained generative model}
\label{sec:rw-adapters}

Given a pretrained model and a new conditioning signal, the methods
available differ along two independent axes. The first is \emph{what is
changed} in the model, which determines what access is required and what a
training step costs. The second is \emph{how the conditioning signal
enters}, which turns out to determine whether it is used at all. We take
them in turn.

\subsection{What is changed}
\label{sec:rw-what-changes}

\paragraph{All parameters.} The baseline is to fine-tune the model, which
requires the weights, the optimiser state for all of them, and a dataset
large enough not to destroy the prior. Low-rank
adaptation~\citep{hu2022lora} reduces the cost by restricting the update to
a low-rank factorisation that can be merged back into the weights at
inference, and the earlier bottleneck adapters of
\citet{houlsby2019adapters} insert small trainable modules between the
frozen layers. Both leave the pretrained values untouched, but both are
still \emph{weight-level} interventions: they require the model to be open
enough to modify, and the forward pass must remain differentiable through
it.

\paragraph{Hidden states.} A second group leaves the weights alone and
instead reads and writes the model's intermediate activations.
ControlNet~\citep{zhang2023controlnet} duplicates the frozen encoder as a
trainable branch and injects its features back through zero-initialised
convolutions. T2I-Adapter~\citep{mou2024t2iadapter} does the same more
cheaply, encoding the condition with a small convolutional network and
adding its features at several scales. IP-Adapter~\citep{ye2023ipadapter}
adds a parallel set of key and value projections beside each cross-attention
layer and sums the two attention outputs. These require access to
activations, and in ControlNet's case to enough of the architecture to
replicate it.

\paragraph{Output only.} The weakest requirement is to treat the model as a
function and modify only what it returns. AVID~\citep{rigter2024avid} does
this: the pretrained video diffusion model is a black box, and a trained
adapter combines its own prediction with the base's through a learned mask.
Nothing inside the model is read or written.

\medskip

The three are ordered by access, and that ordering has a consequence that
\cref{sec:families} develops: only the last is available when the base
cannot be opened, and only the last can be trained without differentiating
through the base.

\paragraph{A shared convention.} Every method above initialises its new
pathway to contribute nothing at the start of training, whether by
near-identity bottlenecks, zero convolutions, zero-initialised output
projections or gated attention with the gate at zero. The justification is
always that an untrained branch should not disturb a working model. Whether
such a pathway subsequently grows to contribute anything is rarely asked;
\citet{zavadski2024controlnetxs} reframe the coupling as a bandwidth
problem and \citet{peng2024controlnext} replace the zero-initialisation with
a normalisation-based alternative, but neither frames the issue as the
conditioning going unused. We return to it in \cref{sec:res-scale}.

\subsection{How the conditioning enters}
\label{sec:rw-conditioning}

Orthogonally to what is changed, a conditioning signal must be combined with
the network's activations somehow, and the available mechanisms are not
interchangeable.

The oldest is feature-wise modulation: a conditioner emits per-channel
scale and shift parameters that are applied to normalised activations. This
was introduced for language-conditioned vision by
\citet{devries2017cbn} and generalised as FiLM by \citet{perez2018film}. In
diffusion models the same construction appears as adaptive group
normalisation~\citep{dhariwal2021adm} and, in transformers, as adaptive
layer normalisation. The alternative is to supply the condition as a
sequence of tokens attended to by the network, which is how latent diffusion
models consume text~\citep{rombach2022ldm}.

\citet{peebles2023dit} compare these directly for class-conditional
generation and find modulation better, by a substantial margin, and cheaper.
\citet{chen2024gentron} compare the same two mechanisms for \emph{text} and
find the reverse, arguing that modulation acts globally on the whole feature
map whereas cross-attention treats spatial positions individually, which
free-form text requires.

The disagreement is instructive rather than contradictory: what differs
between the two studies is the conditioning signal itself, global and
low-dimensional in one case, spatially varying and high-dimensional in the
other. An action vector is squarely in the first regime, and both studies
train the conditioning and the host network together. \Cref{sec:res-pathway}
reports what happens at that point on the axis when the host is frozen.

\section{Reducing the number of sampling steps}
\label{sec:rw-few-step}

Sampling cost is the second obstacle to using a video model as a world
model, and the literature attacks it in three ways that differ in what has
to change.

\paragraph{Better solvers, changing nothing.} The probability-flow ODE of
\cref{sec:rw-generative} can simply be integrated more cleverly.
DDIM~\citep{song2021ddim} constructs a non-Markovian forward process with
the same marginals, giving a deterministic sampler that reuses a trained
model at a fraction of the steps, and DPM-Solver~\citep{lu2022dpmsolver,
lu2022dpmsolverpp} exploits the semi-linear structure of the ODE to reach
comparable quality in around ten. These require no training at all, which
makes them the floor against which any trained method must be measured.

\paragraph{Distilling into a new model.} A second family trains a student to
take larger steps than its teacher. Progressive
distillation~\citep{salimans2022progressive} repeatedly halves the step
count, each round producing a new network. Consistency
models~\citep{song2023consistency} instead train a model to map any point on
a trajectory to its endpoint, so that generation can be done in one step,
and admit both distillation and from-scratch
training~\citep{song2024ict, luo2023lcm}. Distribution-matching
approaches~\citep{yin2024dmd, yin2024dmd2} and adversarial
variants~\citep{sauer2023add, sauer2024ladd} push to one or two steps at the
cost of an adversarial objective. In all of these the prior is replaced.

\paragraph{Conditioning the model on the step size.} A third approach keeps
one network and makes the step size an input, so that a single model can be
asked for a coarse or a fine step at inference. Shortcut
models~\citep{frans2025shortcut} train this way with a self-consistency
objective: the prediction for a step of size $2\dstep$ is supervised against
the composition of two steps of size $\dstep$. The construction is analysed
in \cref{sec:curvature}. \citet{chen2025homo} observe that shortcut training
degrades where the trajectory is strongly curved and propose higher-order
supervision, and \citet{geng2025meanflow} derive the exact relation between
average and instantaneous velocity that the averaging construction
approximates.

\paragraph{Where the acceleration lives.} Cutting across these,
a smaller literature asks whether few-step behaviour can be added to a
model that is kept frozen. LCM-LoRA~\citep{luo2023lcmlora} distils
consistency behaviour into a low-rank module that plugs into unmodified
checkpoints, and \citet{cai2025scfm} shortcut a pretrained flow model with
LoRA on frozen weights, though explicitly \emph{without} an explicit
step-size input. Parallel decoding
distillation~\citep{shaul2026pdd} takes a different route again, emitting
several steps from one network evaluation. This is the group the present
work belongs to, and \cref{sec:res-shortcut} compares the placements
directly.
