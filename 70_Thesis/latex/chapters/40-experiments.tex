\section{The probe suite}
\label{sec:probes}

The readouts normally used to monitor a conditional generative model cannot
report whether the conditioning is used; \cref{sec:res-blindness} exhibits a
case in which they point the wrong way. The measurements in this thesis
therefore rest on instruments built for the question. We describe them here,
together with their nulls and their chance levels, because a probe whose
null is not stated is not a measurement.

\subsection{Three rungs}

The probes form a ladder, and the cells we study \emph{dissociate} along it,
which is why all three rungs are reported rather than a single summary
statistic.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Sensitivity.} Does perturbing the conditioning change the
    prediction at all?
  \item \textbf{Structure.} Does it change it in the \emph{right way} ---
    is the response directional, placed in the frames the action governs,
    and localised on the parts of the scene that move?
  \item \textbf{Control.} Does that structure convert into rollout-level
    trajectory control?
\end{enumerate}

A model can pass the first and fail the second, which is the situation we
find on the flow backbone. Passing the second does not entail the third.

\subsection{Rung 1: action sensitivity}

We measure the relative movement of the prediction under a perturbation of
the action, over predicted frames only:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:effect-rel}
  \mathrm{effect\_rel} \;=\; \frac{\lVert \hat{y}_{\text{true}} - \hat{y}_{\text{pert}} \rVert}{\lVert \hat{y}_{\text{true}} \rVert}.
\end{equation}
The mask matters: diffusion-forcing batches clamp observation frames clean,
and including them dilutes every norm toward zero, which makes an
action-blind model appear weakly sensitive.

Four perturbations are used. \cfg{shuffle} substitutes a different clip's
actions and is the primary readout, because the actions stay on-distribution
and only their correspondence to this clip's dynamics is broken.
\cfg{zero} nulls the actions, but a model trained with condition dropout has
seen zeros and may treat them as a valid token, so a small zero-gap is
weaker evidence than a small shuffle-gap. \cfg{roll} shifts the clip's own
actions in time, separating use of action \emph{magnitude} from use of
action \emph{timing}. \cfg{gauss} substitutes noise matched to the batch
statistics.

Two quantities are always reported alongside, because
\cref{eq:effect-rel} is not interpretable alone: the adapter's relative
contribution $\lVert \hat{y} - \hat{y}_{\text{base}} \rVert / \lVert
\hat{y}_{\text{base}} \rVert$, since an adapter that barely moves the output
has a trivially small action effect; and the loss gap, since a model may
move without moving usefully. We treat a relative movement below
\SI{1}{\percent} as action-blind, applied to the bootstrap confidence
interval rather than the mean, and report \emph{inconclusive} where the
interval straddles it. The threshold is deliberately generous --- far below
what could drive a planner --- so failing it is unambiguous while passing it
establishes little.

\subsection{Rung 2: the structure triad}

\paragraph{Steering direction.} We place clip $B$'s actions on clip $A$'s
noised state and ask whether the prediction moves toward what clip $B$'s
data implies at that state,
\[
  \mathrm{steer} = \cos\big(\hat{y}(a_B) - \hat{y}(a_A),\; \tau(x_B \mid x_t) - \tau(x_A \mid x_t)\big),
\]
with chance at $0$. The target $\tau$ is parameterisation-specific and
getting it wrong silently inverts the probe, so it is never derived locally:
on the diffusion path it is obtained by substitution through the training
objective's own target function, keyed off \cfg{prediction\_type}, and on
the flow path it is the rectified-flow velocity.

\paragraph{Temporal alignment.} We zero the actions in one pixel-frame bin
and measure where the resulting change lands along the \emph{latent} frame
axis. This targets a correspondence that must be learned rather than given:
action tokens are indexed by pixel frame while the backbone operates on
latent frames, which on the flow backbone are temporally compressed. Chance
depends on the number of latent frames and is quoted per cell.

\paragraph{Spatial concentration.} We ask whether the action-driven change
falls on the moving parts of the scene or is spread uniformly; chance is the
mask's area fraction.

\subsection{Controls}

Each probe carries controls, and they are the reason a null result here is
discriminating rather than merely inconclusive.

All variants share one noise draw, and this is enforced rather than assumed:
if the backbone redraws noise per forward, the measured effect is noise
variance, a failure that produces a plausible non-zero number, so the
harness aborts on a mismatch. The frozen base cannot see the action, so its
response must be invariant; we report that as an explicit null and treat any
deviation above a tolerance for non-deterministic kernels as evidence that
conditioning is leaking, in which case the numbers are void rather than
merely noisy. A missing action tensor raises rather than returning zero,
since a preprocessor emitting actions under an unexpected key would leave
the perturbation inert and produce a confident report of action-blindness
that measured nothing. Condition dropout is disabled for probing.

Finally, each probe carries an \emph{empirical} chance estimate alongside
its analytic one, built by breaking the correspondence while preserving the
marginals. The frozen-base null proves the probe measures the adapter, but
because that response is identically zero it provides no spread to compare
against. Where empirical and analytic chance disagree, the analytic level is
wrong and the probe is not read. One consequence is worth recording: the
obvious mismatched pairing for the steering probe is \emph{not} at chance,
because adjacent clip pairs share a term and correlate negatively; the
implementation skips to a disjoint pair.

\section{Methodological integrity}
\label{sec:integrity}

Several measurements taken during this work were invalid, and the campaign
that produced the results in \cref{ch:results} also produced the audits that
retracted them. We record those here rather than in an appendix, because
which numbers were withdrawn --- and how they were caught --- is part of
what the remaining numbers mean.

\paragraph{Evaluation was in-sample.} Two dataset families were evaluated on
the data they were trained on: the jobs pass the same directory as training
and evaluation source, and the trainer's held-out branch does not split it.
Because generative models memorise, a perceptual metric computed this way is
not evidence about generalisation. Every affected number is withheld from
this thesis pending re-evaluation on a held-out split. The environments
carrying the results in \cref{ch:results} use explicit train and test
partitions and are unaffected.

\paragraph{A silent filter discarded most of a dataset.} One cell was
configured for several thousand clips and trained on \num{76}. The window
filter dropped every episode shorter than the configured temporal length,
which on that dataset was almost all of them, and reported nothing. The
result was noticed because it was implausibly good. The entire cell is
retracted, including a headline ratio that had been used to argue that the
dataset sets the level of action-following; that argument does not appear in
this thesis. We now log the retained fraction after every filter.

\paragraph{A capped gate could not move.} The gate cap was set equal to the
gate's initialisation, so the composition weight was frozen for the whole
run. The affected comparison --- an early flow-versus-diffusion consistency
ratio --- was re-run with a live gate, and the re-measurement supersedes it
(\cref{sec:res-shortcut}). We report the superseded figure as superseded
rather than removing it, since the correction is what makes the replacement
credible.

\paragraph{Our first instrument could not distinguish gain from
information.} The relative action effect of \cref{eq:effect-rel} is monotone
in the gain of the action pathway: any intervention that amplifies that
pathway raises the metric whether or not the adapter extracts more
information. Two interventions we had described as mechanism repairs are
themselves gain increases, so the metric could not adjudicate between the
two readings. The resolution was a probe whose null is a \emph{shape} rather
than a magnitude: in a temporal control, the arm without per-frame
conditioning produced per-frame response rows that were bit-identical, a
degeneracy no amount of gain can create. This is why the structure triad,
not \cref{eq:effect-rel}, carries the claims in \cref{ch:results}.

\paragraph{A null that fired, and was not the obvious cause.} On one
backbone the step-size probe's frozen-base null was non-zero, which normally
indicates a conditioning leak and would have voided the affected
measurements. It was instead non-determinism: the frozen base was left in
training mode, so its dropout layers redrew a mask on every forward. The
step-size input is structurally incapable of reaching that base. A control
that fires tells us that something is wrong, not what; diagnosing before
discarding is part of the instrument.

\paragraph{One confound we did not remove.} The cross-backbone shortcut
comparison varies the consistency target and the training depth alongside
the base, so it does not isolate the geometry of the base. The within-arm
comparisons --- each against its own matched control, at matched depth ---
are unaffected, and they are the only form in which we state that result.
The experiment that would resolve it varies one factor and is described in
\cref{sec:future-work}.

\medskip

Four of these were found by audit rather than by a failing test, and three
produced entirely plausible numbers: a metric that moved, an arm that beat
its reference, a ratio that matched a theoretical prediction. Plausibility
is not a check. The practices that caught them were verifying a null rather
than assuming it, reading the evaluation path rather than trusting the flag,
logging what a filter discards, and asking of every metric what \emph{else}
could move it.
