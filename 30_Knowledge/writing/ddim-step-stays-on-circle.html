The DDIM step stays on the circle

A velocity is a tangent. A straight walk along it leaves the manifold (worse with bigger steps). The DDIM step rebalances cos·x + sin·v and rides the arc — at any step size.

DDIM step xφ+Δ = cos(Δ)·xₜ + sin(Δ)·v = ( cos(φ+Δ), sin(φ+Δ) ) — length 1, on the circle
straight walk xₜ + Δ·v — length √(1+Δ²) > 1, off the circle

The cos(Δ)·xₜ term is the whole trick. A straight walk keeps the base point at full weight (1·xₜ + Δ·v) and flies off the tangent line — the error grows with Δ (this is the bias). The DDIM step shrinks the base point by cos(Δ) and grows the velocity by sin(Δ), tracing (cos(φ+Δ), sin(φ+Δ)) — length 1, on the circle, for any Δ. Inversion just picks the velocity that aims this rotation at the true endpoint.

Where the circle comes from — straight out of the noising schedule

  1. The usual forward (noising) process. Variance-preserving diffusion corrupts data as
    xₜ = √ᾱₜ · x₀ + √(1−ᾱₜ) · ε ,   ε ~ 𝒩(0, I)
    with the schedule ᾱₜ = ∏ₛ αₛ running 1 → 0 as t : 0 → T (clean data → pure noise).
  2. Its two coefficients already lie on a circle.
    (√ᾱₜ)² + (√(1−ᾱₜ))² = ᾱₜ + (1−ᾱₜ) = 1
    Two numbers whose squares sum to 1 are the cosine and sine of some angle. Nothing is assumed — this holds identically, for every schedule.
  3. Name that angle. Define the noise angle φₜ = arccos√ᾱₜ, so
    cos φₜ = √ᾱₜ ,  sin φₜ = √(1−ᾱₜ)  ⟹   xₜ = cos φₜ · x₀ + sin φₜ · ε
    As t : 0 → T the schedule drives φ : 0 → π/2 — the point swings from the data axis (x₀) to the noise axis (ε). That sweep is the circle on the canvas.
  4. The velocity is the tangent. Differentiate the arc with respect to its own angle:
    dxₜ/dφ = −sin φₜ · x₀ + cos φₜ · ε =: vₜ
    This is exactly the v-prediction target (Salimans & Ho 2022). "Velocity" is literal — it is the tangent to the noising arc.
  5. A DDIM step just changes φ. Hold the ray (x₀, ε) fixed and move the angle by Δ:
    xφ+Δ = cos(φ+Δ)·x₀ + sin(φ+Δ)·ε = cos Δ · xₜ + sin Δ · vₜ
    — the rotation in the box above. Sampling decreases φ (noise → data); the schedule only sets how far along the arc each timestep moves.

The schedule chooses the spacing of φ along the circle; the circle itself is forced by √ᾱ² + √(1−ᾱ)² = 1. That is why v-prediction lives on an arc — and why averaging two tangents from different angles (the shortcut bias) is a manifold mistake, not a rounding error.
