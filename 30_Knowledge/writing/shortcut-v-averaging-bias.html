The shortcut v-averaging bias

Why (v1+v2)/2 is wrong for diffusion v-prediction — the space misalignment

0One sentence

We add two tangent vectors that live at different points of a curved manifold — an operation the manifold's geometry doesn't define. Doing it in the ambient space silently pretends the local frame didn't rotate between the two points; the leftover rotation (holonomy) is the bias. It's zero for tiny steps and grows with distance along the curve — i.e. worst exactly in the few-/one-step regime D3 cares about.

The honest ambient operation is differencing points (a chord), not adding tangents. That single distinction is the whole story — and it tells you which fixes work (§candidate fixes) and which don't.

Where this is in code. training/shortcut_targets.py:compute_self_consistency_target_v returns ((v1 + v2) / 2).detach(). That averaging is copied from Frans et al. 2024 eq. (4), which is exact for flow matching — but the model here is prediction_type: velocity diffusion stepped by ddim_micro_step_v, a different space. The "paper-faithful" claim holds only as the step size → 0.

1Two objects, both called "velocity"

Flow matching — flat space

Linear interpolant between data x₁ and noise x₀:

x_t = (1−t)·x₀ + t·x₁

The velocity is the constant displacement direction:

v = dx/dt = x₁ − x₀  (same everywhere on the path)

A step is linear: x_{t+d} = x_t + d·v. The trajectory is a straight line.

Diffusion v-pred — curved space

Variance-preserving interpolant (α = ᾱ_t schedule):

x_t = √α_t·x₀ + √(1−α_t)·ε

Set the noise-angle φ = arccos√α_t. Then x_φ = cosφ·x₀ + sinφ·ε — a point on a circle. The v-target is the tangent:

v = √α·ε − √(1−α)·x₀ = dx/dφ (rotates with φ)

A step is a rotation: x_{φ+Δ} = cosΔ·x_φ + sinΔ·v. Trajectory is a circular arc.

The misalignment in one line: flow-matching velocities are parallel along the path, so averaging is exact; diffusion-v tangents rotate along the path, so averaging shrinks and turns them. x₀/ε below are an orthonormal 2-D slice — the geometry generalises to every dimension because the whole step lives in the 2-D plane spanned by x₀ and ε.

◆The real reason — you can't add tangents from different points

The circle picture is one instance of a general fact. The trajectory is a curve on a manifold; v is a tangent to it. v1 and v2 live in different tangent spaces — Tp₀M and Tp₁M — and a manifold has no intrinsic way to add vectors at different points.

The ambient ℝ^d addition v1+v2 is defined — but only because the ambient space hands you a structure the manifold itself doesn't have: the trivial identification of every tangent space (the flat connection). Using it quietly pretends the local frame never rotated between p₀ and p₁. The leftover — the accumulated frame rotation (holonomy) — is the bias, and it grows with arc-distance.

First, forget velocities — just average two points

The bias is there before any tangent talk. Two points on the unit circle, symmetric about 0, separated by total angle 2δ: P₁=(cosδ,−sinδ), P₂=(cosδ,+sinδ). The arc midpoint (on the circle) is x(0)=(1,0). The ambient average is ½(P₁+P₂)=(cosδ,0) — same direction, radius cosδ<1: pulled inward to the chord, off the manifold.

Averaging two unit tangents 2δ apart gives the same cosδ foreshortening — point-average and velocity-average are the same error, related by a derivative.

Why the shrink factor is cos(θ/2)

The shrink is one fact of vector geometry: averaging keeps only the agreement, discards the disagreement.

  • Each vector splits into a part along the bisector (cos(θ/2)) and a part perpendicular (±sin(θ/2)).
  • The perpendicular parts point opposite ways → cancel. Only cos(θ/2) survives. The "lost" length 1−cos(θ/2) is the spread you threw away.
  • θ is the frame rotation (holonomy) between the two points — the arc you crossed. Flat path → θ=0 → cos 0 = 1 → no shrink (why flow matching is exact).
  • Quantitatively, cos(θ/2) = 1 − θ²/8 + O(θ⁴), so the lost length is 1 − cos(θ/2) ≈ θ²/8 — it dies quadratically as the step shrinks. This is precisely the chord-vs-arc (sagitta) discretization error: ≈ κ · (step)² / 8, with curvature κ = 1 on the unit VP circle (and κ = 0 for the straight flow-matching path → exactly zero).
  • Because it scales with step², doubling the step quadruples the shrink: each rung up the self-consistency doubling tower carries ≈ 4× the previous rung's error, so the coarse / one-step rungs dominate. Being O(step²) is also why fine many-step sampling never exposes it — only aggressive few-step does, which is exactly the D3 regime.

In one line: the shrink is the fraction of each vector left over after the frame-rotation (perpendicular) components cancel — the price of a flat average on objects whose frames rotated by θ.

The deepest statement: it's the wrong kind of mean. The arithmetic mean is the Euclidean centroid (minimises straight-line squared distance); the manifold's midpoint is the Riemannian centroid / Fréchet mean (minimises arc-length squared distance). Flat space → they coincide; the circle → Euclidean lands inside at radius cosδ, Riemannian is the arc midpoint. The gap is the sagitta = curvature × step² (κ=1 here; flow matching κ=0 → exact). And it's why differencing points (the honest chord) is fine while averaging (a Euclidean centroid implicitly treated as a manifold point) is not.
So what is the geometry-aware mean — and is it φ-reparam? No. The Riemannian centroid is a point on the circle; computing it needs a map-back onto the manifold. For the shortcut target that map-back is exactly the endpoint composition (follow the flow, re-derive) — Options A/B in §candidate fixes. Making the step grid uniform in φ (Option C) flattens the ladder but supplies no map-back, so it does not turn the Euclidean mean into the Riemannian one — which is why φ-uniform velocity-averaging is still biased (the fixed-point test below).

Two distinct errors (keep them apart)

Addition / holonomy error

The averaged vector is wrong — mostly a rotation of ≈ δ/2 (plus a small cos(δ/2) shrink) — because the frame rotated between the two points. This is "the vector I computed is wrong."

Happens whenever you add tangents from different points. Grows with the arc between them.

Off-manifold drift

Following any tangent as a straight ambient (Euler) step leaves a curved path — the chord departs the arc. This is "even a correct vector, stepped straight, leaves the curve."

A separate, downstream consequence of curvature. On flat space neither error exists.

The honest ambient operation is point-differencing, not tangent-adding. Differencing two points gives a secant/chord, and chords compose exactly: AB⃗ + BC⃗ = AC⃗, always. Adding two tangents from different points does not. Both are "ambient operations," yet one respects the geometry and one doesn't. The shortcut paper parametrises in velocity — which drags the implementation onto the wrong side of that line. You step back over it either by differencing points (predict displacement) or by composing the actual flow (endpoint inversion).

The rigorous test: is the true field a fixed point?

A self-consistency target rule is unbiased iff the true field is a fixed point of its composition. If not, the bootstrap converges to a different, biased field — and the d→0 flow anchor pins the base to truth, so the bias instead accumulates up the doubling tower (more training cannot remove it). Velocity-averaging (½(v_φ+v_{φ+δ}) = cos(δ/2)·v_{φ+δ/2}, rotated δ/2 off the correct v_φ) has residual ≈ 2·sin(δ/2) — the true field is not a fixed point. Differencing points (secants) telescopes — residual exactly 0 — so the true field is a fixed point. This is the manifold story restated as a one-line test.

"But aren't we off-manifold anyway?" — self-consistent ≠ correct

The sagitta argument used exact points. In practice we don't have exact points — we train a network against a d→0 true-field anchor. So the real question is what a model trained toward these targets learns. Grant the steelman:

The objection (valid)

Averaging is off-manifold, but a converged network can drive the loss to zero — g(2d)=avg(g(d)) exactly — a perfectly self-consistent map. So "off-manifold" by itself isn't the indictment.

Why it still fails

Self-consistent ≠ correct for generation. Few-step must match many-step, which needs g(2d) = the velocity that reproduces the two-step endpoint. Averaging gives the shrunk+rotated chord, not that. So the zero-loss model still samples biased at large d.

Flow matching is exempt because there avg = reproducing, exactly (linear step) — self-consistent ⟺ correct. VP/DDIM's cos(θ/2) shrink + rotation splits the two; they only coincide as d→0. The anchor pins the small-d field to the true denoiser, and because the target is .detach()ed, the base / many-step quality is preserved — the bias lives entirely in the large-d few-step tower built above it.

And this is why φ-reparam (§schedule / Option C) is not the cure: uniformising the factor gives a cleaner self-consistent map, but avg is still not the reproducing velocity, so few-step stays biased (the 3–38% is measured in pure uniform-angle). Only endpoint inversion / displacement makes avg = reproducing — that's the fix that matters.

3Why the identical recipe is exact for flow matching

straight-line trajectory v1, v2 (parallel) (v1+v2)/2 correct

In flow matching the path is straight, so v1 and v2 are the same vector. Their average equals each — no shrink, no rotation:

½v1 + ½v2 = v (exact)

and the linear step x + d·v means two d-steps and one 2d-step give the same displacement by construction. The red, green, and blue arrows coincide. That is the whole reason Frans eq. (4) is allowed to average.

The moment you swap the linear step x+d·v for the rotational DDIM step cosΔ·x+sinΔ·v, the arrows stop coinciding — and you are back in section 2.

4The secondary misalignment — timesteps ≠ angle

Section 2 measured the step in arc-angle φ, where the geometry is clean. But the code conditions the model on a step size in timestep units (a normalised t-fraction s) and steps by round(s·T) timesteps. Because φ(t) = arccos√ᾱ_t is nonlinear, a given step size means a different geometric jump depending on where you are on the circle — so the conditioning variable itself, and the half/2× structure the consistency identity rests on, are both distorted.

Linear-β schedule (DynamiCrafter: β ∈ [0.00085, 0.012], T=1000). The dashed marks show that the timestep midpoint of a jump is far from its angle midpoint near the noisy end.

The step-size conditioning is geometrically ambiguous

The model conditions on a step size d in timestep units — but d is not a fixed geometric jump. The arc swept per unit t is

dφ/dt = β(t)/2 · cot φ

which runs ≈ 5.7× faster near the clean end than the noisy end (DynamiCrafter, zero-terminal-SNR). So the same conditioned d means a big arc near clean, a tiny arc near noise — one scalar, many different jumps.

That breaks the doubling the consistency identity needs: the supervised 2d and its two halves (at t and t−d) sweep different arcs that don't sum to the 2d arc — the "halving" is warped, worst where dφ/dt changes fastest.

Fix: condition on (and grid in) arc-length φ or log-SNR λ. Then a given step size always sweeps the same arc, halving is a true geometric halving, and the conditioning is unambiguous. Still secondary — stack it on endpoint/displacement; it doesn't touch the primary averaging bias.

Key reframe: the bias is a rotation (≈ δ/2), not a shrink (‖avg‖ = cos(δ/2) ≈ 0.92–1.0). That is why a scalar correction (Sol 3) is a no-op — its landing error is identical to the uncorrected average to the decimal.

✓ Endpoint inversion — start here

Follow the ODE both sub-steps to the real landing x_end, then invert the DDIM step for the single-2δ velocity that hits it. Exact (true field is a fixed point). Target-only change; keeps the velocity head + DDIM sampler; schedule-faithful. Least invasive. Detailed below.

✓ Predict displacement (Sol 2)

Output net displacement and compose additively: Δ(2d)=Δ(d)+Δ′(d) — point-differencing telescopes exactly, schedule-independent. The same fix in displacement coordinates. Cost: reparametrise the head and the shortcut sampler; base case grounds on ≈ d·v_true. Best if the β-schedule isn't a clean circle.

~ Arc-length reparam (Sol 1) — secondary, stack it

Condition / sample uniform in the angle φ. Fixes the secondary misalignment (§schedule: timesteps ≠ angle) — worth doing on top of a real fix. But it doesn't change the composition rule, so velocity-averaging's biased fixed point persists (pure uniform-angle still 3–38% off). "Uniform holonomy → absorbable scalar" fails: the discrepancy is a rotation, and the true field isn't a fixed point, so it compounds rather than absorbs.

✕ Scalar correction (Sol 3) — reject

sin(δ/2)/(δ/2)·½(v1+v2). A scalar fixes only magnitude; the dominant error is the δ/2 rotation. Landing error identical to the uncorrected average (table). A no-op — not even a useful ablation.

✓ Use a flow-matching base — the structural escape

The bias is curvature. A flow-matching model has κ=0 (straight path) → avg = reproducing, sagitta ≡ 0, no fix needed at all. Caveat: this is a backbone choice (DynamiCrafter is v-pred diffusion), not a loss tweak — but the framework spans both (D1), so it's a legitimate, clean sidestep.

"Flatten the space" was the right instinct — but the flattening that works is displacement / endpoint space, not angle-time. Angle-time removes a different, smaller curvature (the schedule's), not the tangent-averaging curvature.

6The fix in detail — endpoint inversion

Stop comparing tangents. Compare where you land. The endpoint of the trajectory is a frame-independent point on the circle; the tangent is frame-dependent (it rotates). Enforcing consistency on the endpoint is exact at every step size — that is the consistency-models philosophy, transplanted onto your shortcut adapter.

Recipe

  1. First sub-step (already done). v1 = model(x_t, t, d); step to x_mid = DDIM(x_t, v1, t → t−d).
  2. Second sub-step (new). v2 = model(x_mid, t−d, d); take the second step too — x_end = DDIM(x_mid, v2, t−d → t−2d). The current code stops at x_mid and never forms x_end — that omission is the bug.
  3. Invert (new). Solve for the single velocity at (x_t, t) whose one 2d DDIM step lands on x_end. Detach; that is the target.

The math

The DDIM step is invertible because it is affine in v. With α₀ = ᾱ_t, α₂ = ᾱ_{t−2d} (and √(1−α) the noise weights), the recompose from (x_t, v) to the 2d landing is:

DDIM₂(v) = (√(α₂α₀) + √((1−α₂)(1−α₀)))·x_t + (√((1−α₂)α₀) − √(α₂(1−α₀)))·v

Set DDIM₂(v) = x_end and solve:

v_target = ( x_end − (√(α₂α₀) + √((1−α₂)(1−α₀)))·x_t ) / ( √((1−α₂)α₀) − √(α₂(1−α₀)) )

This is the same decompose-recompose your ddim-step-v-parameterisation.md derives (eq. D), read backwards. Equivalently: decode x_end and x_t to clean-data estimates (x̂₀, ε̂) — the frame-independent coordinates — and read off the velocity that connects them; the two views give the same v_target. Compare to the buggy target (v1+v2)/2, which on a consistent ray equals cos(δ/2)·v_{φ+δ/2} — shrunk by cos(δ/2) and rotated by δ/2 off the correct v_φ.

"But you can't invert a big jump — isn't DDIM only infinitesimal?" No. The DDIM step is not an Euler translation x + v·d (that would leave a curved manifold). It is a finite rotation — x_next = cos(2δ)·x + sin(2δ)·v — and the cos(2δ)·x term bends it back onto the manifold, exact at any jump size (which is why 4-step sampling works at all). Inverting a rotation is trivial and unique: for any endpoint C there is exactly one v = (C − cos(2δ)·x)/sin(2δ). Edge cases behave — 2δ→0 is a 0/0 → the true instantaneous velocity (and the smallest step is the anchor, never inverted); large 2δ is better-conditioned (sin>0 on (0,π)). The "extend a tangent and you leave the manifold" worry is the Euler/displacement picture — exactly what Option B embraces with honest chords. DDIM rotates; displacement translates.

The change in code

current — training/shortcut_targets.py

def compute_self_consistency_target_v(*, model, x_t, t, cond_half, d, alphas_cumprod, scale_arr):
    model.eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        v1 = model(x_t, t, cond_half)
        prev_t = (t - d).clamp_min(0)
        x_mid  = ddim_micro_step_v(x=x_t, v=v1, t=t, prev_t=prev_t, ...)
        v2 = model(x_mid, prev_t, cond_half)
    return ((v1 + v2) / 2.0).detach()        # <- averages tangents on a curved manifold

corrected — endpoint-space target

def compute_self_consistency_target_v(*, model, x_t, t, cond_half, d, alphas_cumprod, scale_arr):
    model.eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        v1 = model(x_t, t, cond_half)
        prev_t  = (t -   d).clamp_min(0)
        prev_t2 = (t - 2*d).clamp_min(0)
        x_mid = ddim_micro_step_v(x=x_t,  v=v1, t=t,      prev_t=prev_t,  alphas_cumprod=alphas_cumprod, scale_arr=scale_arr)
        v2    = model(x_mid, prev_t, cond_half)
        # NEW: actually take the second sub-step to the true 2d landing
        x_end = ddim_micro_step_v(x=x_mid, v=v2, t=prev_t, prev_t=prev_t2, alphas_cumprod=alphas_cumprod, scale_arr=scale_arr)
        # NEW: invert the SAME DDIM recompose for the one-step-2d velocity that hits x_end
        v_target = invert_ddim_v(x_t, x_end, t, prev_t2, alphas_cumprod, scale_arr)
    return v_target.detach()

invert_ddim_v must mirror exactly what ddim_micro_step_v does. With scale_arr=None it is the closed form above. With dynamic rescale (scale_arr set), ddim_micro_step_v multiplies pred_x0 by prev_scale/cur_scale before recompose, so the inversion must divide it back out — cleanest is to invert via the decoded (x̂₀, ε̂) so the rescale cancels on both sides rather than hand-deriving a second formula.

Implementation checklist

  • Add the second ddim_micro_step_v call → x_end.
  • Add invert_ddim_v (closed form for scale_arr=None; rescale-aware via (x̂₀,ε̂) otherwise).
  • Guard the denominator √((1−α₂)α₀) − √(α₂(1−α₀)): it shrinks toward 0 only as 2d → 0, and the smallest step is the anchor (handled by the standard loss, never routed here) — but clamp for safety.
  • Leave the anchor path untouched: at d → 0 the old and new targets already coincide.
  • No extra model forward. The two forwards (v1, v2) are already paid; the fix adds one cheap DDIM micro-step (x_end) + an algebraic inversion on tensors you already hold. Negligible vs the gradient step.

Regression test

  • On a synthetic (x₀,ε) ray, assert one 2d DDIM step with v_target reproduces the two-step landing to <1e-5 — for several step sizes and under a perturbed v2 (the 0.000000 column in §5).
  • Assert the old (v1+v2)/2 target fails that same check at the 2-step rung (≈16%), to lock in the contrast.
  • Pair with the per-rung logging: coarse rungs should now converge, not plateau.
Why it matters for the thesis. It removes a systematic cap on few-step quality — the D3 headline — by making the self-consistency objective's fixed point the true velocity field instead of a biased one. And it corrects the "paper-faithful" wording: under v-prediction, faithful means endpoint-consistent, not v-averaged. Frans eq. (4) is recovered exactly in the d → 0 / flow-matching limit.

Equivalent fix: predict the net displacement (Option B)

Instead of inverting back to a velocity, change what the head outputs: the net displacement Δ (the chord to the next point), composed additively Δ(2d) = Δ(d) + Δ′(d), and sample by adding displacements x_next = x + Δ. Differencing points gives honest chords, and chords telescope exactly — the intermediate point cancels — for any curvature or schedule.

Same endpoint-consistency as inversion, in displacement coordinates: exact and schedule-independent. Cost: reparametrise the head's output and the shortcut sampler (add displacements rather than DDIM-recompose), and ground the d→0 displacement on ≈ d·v_true. Pick this over inversion if you don't trust the β-schedule to be a clean uniform-angle circle.

…or sidestep it entirely: use a flow-matching base

The structural escape. The bias is curvature — κ=1 on the VP circle. A flow-matching model has κ=0: a straight path, so avg = reproducing and the sagitta is identically zero. Swap the frozen base from v-prediction diffusion to flow matching and the whole bug disappears — no target surgery, no inversion, no reparametrisation, eq. (4) is exact as written. The cost is that this is a backbone-level decision (DynamiCrafter is v-pred diffusion), so it means adopting a flow-matching video backbone (or reparametrising the base) rather than touching the loss. But the thesis framework already spans diffusion and flow matching (D1) — so "just be on a straight line" is a legitimate, and arguably the cleanest, way to avoid D3's sharpest pitfall.
