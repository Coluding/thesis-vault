How the rescaling lands back on the circle

Start at xₜ, read off the velocity v (the tangent). Scale the base point by cos Δ, scale the velocity by sin Δ, add them — and because xₜ ⊥ v with equal length, the corner lands exactly on the circle.

xt′ = cos Δ · xₜ + sin Δ · v |xₜ| = |v| = R and xₜ ⊥ v  ⟹  |xt′| = R·√(cos²Δ + sin²Δ) = R  → on the circle, every Δ.

The base point is the half that matters. The red dashed arrow is where the velocity points — follow it fully and you land at xₜ + v, off the circle at √2·R. The fix isn't to the velocity alone: you also shrink the base point to cos Δ · xₜ. Those two rescaled, perpendicular, equal-length legs form a right triangle whose hypotenuse from O is always exactly R. Slide to Δ = 90°: cos Δ → 0 kills the base point entirely and the answer is the (full) velocity — still on the circle. Slide to Δ = 0°: sin Δ → 0, no move, you stay at xₜ.
