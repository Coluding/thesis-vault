Fixing the shortcut target: rotate vs. translate

Two exact, endpoint-consistent fixes for the biased v-averaging shortcut target — and where they actually differ.

Both fixes reach the same destination — the true two-step landing point x₂d — so both are exact. They differ in the route: A · inversion keeps the velocity head and the curved DDIM step (rotate, stay on the arc); B · displacement switches the head to secants and adds them (translate, cut across as chords). The biased baseline averages two tangents and lands inside the arc.

A — Endpoint inversion · rotate

The step is a rotation x₂d = cos(2δ)·xₜ + sin(2δ)·v, so it stays on the manifold for any jump size.
target v = (x₂d − cos(2δ)·xₜ) / sin(2δ)

Touches: the target only. Head still predicts a velocity; DDIM sampler unchanged.

Catch: invert_ddim_v must mirror the stepper exactly, incl. scale_arr.

B — Displacement · translate

Secants add tip-to-tail; the intermediate point cancels, regardless of curvature or schedule.
target Δ(2d) = Δ(d) + Δ′(d) = x₂d − xₜ

Touches: the head (predicts Δ) and the sampler (adds displacements).

Catch: bigger change; d→0 grounds on ≈ d·v_true.

Bottom line. Same endpoint, different coordinate: A rotates (keep velocity + DDIM, swap only the target), B translates (move to displacement space, add the steps). Both are exact fixed points of the self-consistency rule; the discarded v-average baseline is not — it lands inside the arc by the sagitta. Shipping both, config-selectable, gives the D3 ablation axis: average vs. inversion vs. displacement.
