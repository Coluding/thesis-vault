## 16:17 — Param-matched comparison across adapter families

Idea: run a head-to-head comparison between adapter approaches (full
finetuning, LoRA, hypernetwork, AVID) at a **matched trainable-parameter
budget**, rather than at each method's "natural" default.

Motivating observation: the current AVID-style setup has only ~0.78% of
backbone params trainable. A fair comparison should hold that budget
roughly constant across families so any difference in prediction
accuracy / stability / inference cost can be attributed to *where* the
capacity is spent (low-rank weight delta vs. hidden-state injection vs.
hypernetwork-generated delta vs. output-level residual), not to *how
much* capacity each gets.

Open questions to resolve before this becomes a real protocol:

- What exactly does "matched params" mean? Trainable params total?
  Trainable params per block? FLOPs at inference? Memory footprint?
- Do we match at AVID's 0.78%, or sweep across (e.g.) 0.1% / 0.8% / 3%
  to see whether the ranking is budget-dependent?
- What axes do we hold fixed (backbone, dataset, action conditioning
  interface, training steps, eval protocol)?
- Does the comparison live inside D1 (framework characterisation) or
  D2 (action-conditioned world model results)?
